The problem

Reading about Bitcoin isn't the same as using it.

You've heard about Bitcoin. Maybe you've read some articles or books, or even bought some on an exchange. But when it comes to actually using it — something stops you.

What if it's too complicated? What if you mess up?

Mistakes in Bitcoin can be costly, but they're also 100% avoidable with the right guidance. And of course, the biggest mistake is to own no bitcoin at all, and miss out on this revolutionary new form of money.

There are mountains of videos, podcasts, and books about why Bitcoin matters. What's missing is clear, practical, hands-on practice in the basics of using bitcoin without middlemen — not on an exchange like Coinbase, not through an ETF. That's called self custody: you store, send, and receive bitcoin yourself, privately and securely, without asking any institution for permission.

The method

Roll up your sleeves and use it — in a risk-free environment.

Think back to learning to drive. Did your instructor start with the physics of internal combustion engines? No. They said: "Here's the gas pedal, here's the brake. Let's practice in this empty parking lot. Now try turning — see, you're doing it!"

Bitcoin Hands-On works the same way. After installing Sparrow Wallet (a well-known, free and open-source Bitcoin wallet), you'll work through exercises like these:

  1. Buy a tiny amount of real bitcoin from a trusted exchange
  2. Set up your first real wallets
  3. Prove to yourself your wallet can be recovered
  4. Send and receive bitcoin securely and confidently
  5. Hold bitcoin long-term in self custody
  6. Spot and avoid scammers and other dangers

The potential pitfalls are laid out in advance, so you steer clear of them. And every exercise ends with a "Learn More" section — there if you're curious about the theory, skippable if you just want the essentials.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I see a few sample exercises first?

Sure. Download the sample chapters — they'll give you a good idea of exactly how the exercises work.

Do I have to buy real bitcoin to do the exercises?

Yes. To go through all the exercises, you'll need a very small amount of real bitcoin. A purchase of $25 is plenty to do everything, including fees, with most of it left over — and the book guides you through the purchase.

What do I need to get started?

About $25 to buy some bitcoin, a computer (Windows, Mac, or Linux), a mobile phone, and the willingness to learn some new software.

Will I learn the theory too, or is it all exercises?

The book is focused on practical understanding. But every exercise ends with a "Learn More" section that goes deeper into the theory and terminology — private keys, mining, fees, and so on — behind what you just did.

Does it cover other cryptocurrencies, or just Bitcoin?

Just Bitcoin. Honestly, Bitcoin is the only one that's viable over the long term. By focusing on Bitcoin alone, you master the one that started it all and has proven itself over time.

Resources

Books

The Bitcoin Standard — Saifedean Ammous
The classic that everyone even remotely interested in Bitcoin should read. Ammous explores what makes a good money, the best monies throughout history, and how those holding the old money are impoverished when a new one replaces it.
Broken Money — Lyn Alden
Alden leverages her background as an investment strategist to expose the cracks in the global financial system — how fiat currencies, central banks, and money printing fuel inequality and inflation — and argues for Bitcoin as the fix.
Inventing Bitcoin — Yan Pritzker
A lot of helpful information packed into a short book. Clear, direct explanations of cryptography, decentralization, mining, proof-of-work, and the incentives that help the Bitcoin network thrive.

Articles

The Bullish Case for Bitcoin — Vijay Boyapati
The short article that has introduced many people to the "why" of bitcoin, including a review of money throughout history and why Bitcoin is superior to government currency — and even gold.
Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System — Satoshi Nakamoto
The original 9-page white paper that started it all. Two months after it appeared, bitcoin mining began.
Bitcoin First Revisited — Fidelity Digital Assets
Positions Bitcoin as the premier digital money and explains the risks of chasing altcoins: centralization, regulatory issues, and less proven networks.
Bitcoin Magazine
One of the original sources for all things Bitcoin, since 2012. In-depth articles, news, and analysis. (It's ad-supported — keep that in mind as you read.)

YouTube

BTC Sessions
Beginner-friendly practical tutorials on wallets, security, self-custody, and the Lightning Network.
Bitcoin University
Matthew Kratter's frequent, straightforward videos on Bitcoin as an investment, plus self-custody information.
Aantonop
Andreas M. Antonopoulos, author of Mastering Bitcoin and one of the earliest Bitcoin educators. Not very active now, but full of classics.

More

Learn Me A Bitcoin
Easy-to-understand, graphical explanations of the important concepts in Bitcoin, built by Greg Walker since 2015.
Bitcoin meetups
Meeting bitcoiners face to face is a great way to learn and hear what's going on locally. Stick with Bitcoin-only meetups and skip the "crypto" ones.
X (formerly Twitter)
Many bitcoiners are active on X. Start by following your favorite content creators — but understand that replies on popular accounts are often polluted with scammers.
